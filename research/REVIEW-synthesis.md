# Independent review of `SYNTHESIS.md`

Reviewer: independent adversarial review, 2026-11-13. Scope: `research/SYNTHESIS.md` checked against the topic notes and `SOURCES.md`, plus web spot-checks of the sources the recommendation depends on most. I edited no other file.

## Summary verdict

**Sound in outline, but not yet safe to build on.** Most citations hold up. Every source I spot-checked says what the synthesis says it says, apart from small wording slips. Two problems go to the core of the product, though, and neither is visible in the synthesis:

1. **The "who owes a reply" model is one-sided.** Under the classical burden rule only the claimant ever owes a reply. So the headline obligations view will consistently show the person defending a thesis (in a faith debate, usually the believer) as the one "owing", and the doubter as owing nothing. That amounts to a quiet judgement, which the owner's rule 5 forbids. The model also has no rule for mixed disputes, where both friends hold opposite theses.
2. **The transport design lets any link holder wipe the relay mailbox for good.** Every event is signed with one key derived from the shared link secret. Nostr's NIP-62 ("request to vanish") lets any holder of that key ask relays to delete everything and *refuse re-broadcast*. That disables the anti-entropy republish that the whole durability argument relies on.

The privacy framing ("consistent with no tracking") and the durability claims also rest on thinner evidence than the synthesis lets on. There are internal tensions on move-set complexity and the JS budget. Several gaps that matter for this owner (identity across devices, link leak and revocation, multi-party debates, screen readers in Arabic, relay terms of service) are not raised at all.

## Gaps that matter

The gaps are ranked by how much they would hurt this owner's use case.

1. **Mixed disputes and the questioner's obligations** (F1). This is the core feature; the model has to be designed before the UI.
2. **Revocation and transport-key design** (F2, F7): what happens when a link leaks or a friendship sours mid-debate. That means re-keying, removing a participant, and stopping one key holder from wiping relays.
3. **Identity across devices.** A phone and a laptop belong to one person, and phones get replaced over months. Authorship drives "who owes", so this cannot be left to "optional signatures".
4. **Multi-party debates (> 2).** Munazara is defined for two people (MUN §0). A WhatsApp friend group has 3–6. Who is the sāʾil of a thesis when three people challenge it? Does each challenger's open item count separately? Even if v1 is two-party, the data model should not rule out more.
5. **Relay viability over time** (F4): a soak test, NIP-11 limitation fields, and anti-spam rules (web-of-trust, NIP-42 AUTH, NIP-13 PoW).
6. **Screen readers in Arabic.** UX mentions screen-reader text only in passing. There is no research on VoiceOver or TalkBack with Arabic and mixed-direction content (`<bdi>`, `dir="auto"`), on announcing status badges and obligation counts, or on focus order in a focus-view tree. WCAG 1.4.1 is covered; 4.1.2 and 1.3.1 are not.
7. **Moderation of abuse between friends.** Faith debates get heated. There is no mechanism beyond an ethics charter: no way to hide a hurtful node for oneself, pause a debate or leave cleanly. Given "never judges", this must be self-moderation, but it needs designing.
8. **Data export format.** The synthesis mentions a backup file but no open, documented, human-readable export (for example JSON plus a printable or Markdown transcript). That matters for "long-lived": the debate should outlive the app.
9. **Legal and terms-of-service position** of storing data on public relays and of GitHub Pages' acceptable use for an app whose purpose is storage elsewhere (TECH §8 gives "our reading; not a legal opinion").
10. **Dialect speech-to-text** (F17) and the **migration path from voice notes**. The owner's current medium is voice. "Text only" is a large behaviour change, and its acceptability is untested.

Lower priority: Arabic labels for moves (already flagged in §8), long WhatsApp links (already flagged), and the Moonshine licence (irrelevant for v1).
